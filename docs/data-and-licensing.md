# Data and licensing

**This page is not legal advice.** It describes what queuetruth does with data and summarizes vendor terms as we read
them on 2026-10-18. Your licence with your data vendor or exchange governs what you may do with their data
and with anything you derive from it. Read it, and ask your counsel when in doubt.

## The software's licence

queuetruth is licensed under the Apache License 2.0 (`LICENSE`). `NOTICE` and `third_party/licenses/` credit the code
it ports: queue-model logic from hftbacktest (MIT). NautilusTrader (LGPL-3.0) behaviour is re-implemented from its
public documentation, with no NautilusTrader source included (the cross-check script `scripts/nt_crosscheck` calls
the released package's public Python API in a separate environment and is not shipped in the wheel), and Databento's queue-position tutorial estimator is
re-implemented from its documented behaviour. R `cmprsk` (GPL) was used only to generate committed reference numbers
for a test; it is not part of the package.

## What queuetruth does with data

- **It ships no market data.** No ITCH, DBN, Tardis, Coinbase, Bitstamp or Bitfinex records, not even excerpts, are
  in the repository, the wheels, the images or these docs. Test fixtures are generated by our own code; importer
  fixtures are written from public format documentation with invented accounts and values.
- **It never downloads data by itself.** The one download helper (`scripts/fetch_nasdaq_itch.py`) runs only when a
  person passes `--yes`. The docs link to the Nasdaq sample directory instead of mirroring files.
- **It makes no network calls when run locally.** No telemetry, no licence checks, no update checks. The CLI and the
  MCP server call out only to a queuetruth service URL you configure (`QT_API_URL`).
- **It never asks for or stores vendor credentials** (Databento, Tardis or exchange keys), locally or in the service.
- **Your data stays where you run it.** Local runs read and write only the paths you give. Nothing leaves your machine
  unless you submit a job to a service yourself.

## Zero-egress operation

- Local CLI and Python API: no network access at all.
- Self-hosted service with `QT_ZERO_EGRESS=true` (or zero egress enabled for a tenant): job processes can open
  connections only to the database and the object store; any other connection raises; the billing adapter is forced
  off; `GET /v1/meta` reports the mode. This is tested (O-EGRESS-1). See
  [Self-host the service](how-to/self-host-the-service.md).

## What the outputs contain (treat them accordingly)

| Output | Contains | Treat as |
|---|---|---|
| ingested dataset (`mbo/`, `l2/`, `trades_source/`) | every source record, reformatted | the raw data itself |
| replay tables (`trades`, `bbo`, `depth`, `rank_trace`, `real_orders`, `l2/`) | event-level records and prices | raw data |
| shadow and zoo tables (`shadow_fills`, `queue_trajectory`, `zoo_predictions`, `zoo_fills`, features) | per-order event times, prices, queue sizes | derived at event level; usually still a substitute for the source |
| `summary.json`, `calibration.json`, model cards | counts, parameters, error metrics, intervals | aggregated derived data |
| `model.pkl` (learned model) | a model trained on the data | a model developed with the data (some vendors restrict these, below) |
| HTML report | aggregates **and** event-level "why" traces, position series, markout inputs | contains market data: remove traces (`--max-why 0`) before sharing outside your licence |
| live log (`live/`) and scorecards | your orders and fills, account ids, raw venue messages in `raw` | your confidential data; drop the `raw` columns before sharing |

Reformatting into Parquet does not change what the data is: some vendor terms say explicitly that data that is only
reformatted, filtered or sampled is not derived data.

## Common uses and what to check

| Use | Typical licence position | What to check |
|---|---|---|
| Run queuetruth on data you licensed, for your own research and backtests | internal use, generally permitted | that your plan covers non-display or research use |
| Share a report or tables inside your firm | internal use | user or device limits of your licence |
| Send a report to a vendor, broker, client or consultant | external distribution of derived information | your vendor's redistribution terms; Coinbase bars derived works to third parties |
| Publish aggregate model-error statistics (what the project does with Nasdaq sample days) | usually acceptable for aggregates that cannot reconstruct the data and are not price data | name the source; link, never mirror; no event-level rows |
| Publish event-level replay traces, per-order "why" timelines or example datasets | allowed for IEX HIST with IEX's attribution line; not for Nasdaq samples or licensed vendor data | carry the IEX attribution on every artifact (below) |
| Use Binance Vision archive files (`data.binance.vision`) | non-commercial only (CC BY-NC-SA 4.0) | no production calibration, no commercial products, no hosting (so not the hosted service); derived works must keep the same licence |
| Publish or sell fitted parameters or a fill model | often restricted | Tardis restricts models developed with its data to internal use; Coinbase bars derived works to third parties; Databento requires distribution rights and attribution; CME requires a derived-data agreement for external derivative works |

## Vendor notes

Short summaries of the terms as we read them on 2026-10-18. Follow the links for the current text.

- **Nasdaq TotalView-ITCH public samples** ([emi.nasdaq.com/ITCH](https://emi.nasdaq.com/ITCH/Nasdaq%20ITCH/)). We
  found no terms of use specific to the sample directory, and Nasdaq claims copyright. Nasdaq's data policies define
  derived data as information that cannot be reverse engineered into the source or used as a substitute for it, and
  treat derived data that contains prices as generally fee-liable. Nasdaq has a declaration form for derived-data
  uses ([US Equities and Options Data Policies](https://www.nasdaqtrader.com/content/AdministrationSupport/Policy/USEquitiesandOptionsDataPolicies.pdf)).
  The project links to the files and publishes only aggregate statistics.
- **Databento** ([user agreement](https://databento.com/legal/databento-user-agreement),
  [pricing](https://databento.com/pricing)). The licence is for internal business purposes, used by the customer's
  employees and contractors acting for the customer. "Redistribution" covers information derived from the data;
  redistribution must follow the publisher's terms and carry "Data Provided by Databento" attribution, and external
  distribution comes with the higher plans. The agreement also restricts service-bureau use of the platform.
- **Tardis.dev** ([terms of service](https://docs.tardis.dev/legal/terms-of-service)). Permitted use is internal
  business, research, educational or personal use. Derived data excludes data that was only reformatted, filtered or
  sampled. Models developed or improved with Tardis data may be used internally, including for backtesting and
  market simulation, but may not be provided to third parties, even when they cannot reconstruct the data. Data
  disclosed to service providers must be covered by flow-down terms.
- **Coinbase** ([market data terms](https://www.coinbase.com/legal/market_data)). Market data is for your own or your
  organisation's personal or research purposes; data, analytics or other works derived from it may not be given to
  third parties outside your organisation without consent.
- **CME** (fee list and derived-data schedules; cmegroup.com was not reachable for a full read). Derivative works are
  licensed for internal business purposes; creating derivative works for external distribution needs a Derived Data
  License Agreement. Whether non-price statistical parameters count as derivative works is not stated in what we
  could read.
- **IEX HIST** ([HIST downloads](https://iextrading.com/trading/market-data/),
  [IEX Historical Data Terms of Use](https://iextrading.com/iex-historical-data-terms/)). Free daily pcap files, T+1,
  including the order-by-order DEEP+ feed since 2024-10-01. Using the data means agreeing to IEX's terms. Their
  condition for distributing, selling, furnishing or otherwise providing access to the data is that you cite IEX as
  the source with this exact text and a link to the terms:

  > Data provided for free by IEX. By accessing or using IEX Historical Data, you agree to the IEX Historical Data
  > Terms of Use.

  IEX keeps its proprietary rights, and the data covers trading on IEX only. These are the most permissive terms we
  found for real order-level data, which makes IEX DEEP+ the intended public demo source: event-level traces and
  example datasets built from it can be published when every artifact carries the line above. queuetruth
  reads DEEP+ pcap files (`ingest --format iex-deep-plus`, rule set `iex`; see
  [Replay a public IEX DEEP+ day](how-to/iex-public-demo.md)), and `scripts/iex/stream_subset.py` records the
  attribution line in its subset manifest. DEEP+ does not show non-displayed orders, so their executions appear as
  trades without a resting order, which maps onto queuetruth's hidden-print handling.
- **Binance Vision** ([binance-public-data terms](https://github.com/binance/binance-public-data/blob/master/TERMS_AND_CONDITIONS.md),
  added 2026-09-30). The public archive (`data.binance.vision`: trades, aggregated trades and klines, no order book)
  is licensed under Creative Commons Attribution-NonCommercial-ShareAlike 4.0, and commercial licensing is excluded.
  Permitted uses are limited to academic research, non-monetized open educational projects, personal non-production
  backtesting and non-commercial open-source evaluation. Hosting, selling or commercially exploiting the data or
  derived feeds is prohibited, and so is integrating it into commercial financial products or trading tools.
  Derivative works must carry attribution and stay under the same licence. In practice: do not calibrate a
  production fill model on Binance Vision files, do not upload them to a hosted service (including a hosted
  queuetruth), and licence anything you publish from them under CC BY-NC-SA 4.0. (The repository's own code is MIT;
  the datasets are not.) Binance L2 order-book history from Tardis falls under the Tardis terms above, not these.
- **Coinbase and Kraken L3 captures.** Coinbase Exchange's `full`, `level2` and `level3` WebSocket channels now
  require authentication ([Coinbase WebSocket authentication](https://docs.cdp.coinbase.com/exchange/websocket-feed/authentication));
  only `level2_batch` does not. Kraken's spot `level3` channel is authenticated too
  ([Kraken level3](https://docs.kraken.com/api/docs/websocket-v2/level3)). A capture you record yourself therefore
  comes under your account's API terms as well as the market-data terms, and we have not re-read those API terms for
  storage or redistribution. Two data consequences: Coinbase's authenticated messages do not increment the
  sequence number, so dropped authenticated messages cannot be detected from the sequence; and Kraken L3 is exact
  only within the subscribed depth (10, 100 or 1,000 levels), sends no delete when a level leaves that window, adds
  orders again when they re-enter it, and excludes in-flight orders, unmatched market orders, untriggered stops and
  hidden iceberg quantity. queuetruth reads both captures (`coinbase-level3`, `kraken-l3`; see
  [Ingest order-level crypto feeds](how-to/crypto-l3-sources.md)).
- **Bitstamp, Bitfinex.** We found no primary market-data redistribution terms. Ask the venue.

## Third-party capture and normalization tools

queuetruth does not depend on cryptofeed or any other capture library; its readers parse the capture formats
themselves. If you record crypto feeds with **cryptofeed**, note that it changed to the GNU Affero General Public
License (AGPL-3.0-or-later) in v3 (September 2026), after a permissive licence in earlier versions. The AGPL's
network clause matters if you embed it in a service that others use over a network; check with your counsel before
doing that. The capture files it writes are your data and are not affected by queuetruth's Apache-2.0 licence.

## Record the data vintage

Vendors re-issue history, and the same file name can hold different data later. Two current examples:

- **Databento re-issues its Nasdaq history (XNAS.ITCH) on 2026-10-31**, retroactively
  ([Databento announcement](https://databento.com/blog/nasdaq-historical-data-changes-2026-09)): instrument ids
  before 2023-11-28 are reassigned; after-hours data during US standard time, which used to stop at 00:00 UTC, now
  runs to 01:00 UTC, so each UTC date starts with the final hour of the previous session; and MBO, definition and
  status files gain midnight-UTC snapshot records. A replay of a pre-re-issue file and of a post-re-issue file of
  the same day will differ.
- **DBN version 4** is in development, with a changed record header. The v0.1 reader is built on the `dbn` 0.71
  crate and decodes DBN versions 1 to 3; expect DBN v4 files to need a reader update.

What to do:

- For every input, keep the vendor, dataset, download date, the vendor's gateway or version where it has one, the
  DBN version for DBN files, and the file's sha256. queuetruth records the path, size and sha256 of every input in
  the dataset's `manifest.json` (`inputs`), and the `dataset_id` and every `run_id` change when any input byte
  changes, so a result can always be traced to the exact files. The vendor, download date and gateway are not
  recorded by queuetruth; keep them next to the files.
- Do not mix vintages in one dataset or one comparison (for example pre- and post-2026-10-31 XNAS.ITCH files), and
  re-run calibrations when you re-download history.
- Join instruments across days by symbol, not by instrument id: ids are per day on ITCH and are being reassigned on
  Databento's re-issue.
- Verify the checksums a source publishes (Nasdaq EMI, IEX file sizes, Binance Vision CHECKSUM files) before
  ingesting.

## The hosted service (if you use it)

No hosted queuetruth service is offered today. These are the rules a hosted service would follow; the
self-hosted service already implements the technical ones (tenant isolation, no pooling, retention, no vendor
credentials).

- You upload only data you are licensed to process, and you confirm that you are. The service processes it only to
  run the jobs you submit and returns results only to you, acting as your processor.
- **No pooling.** No endpoint aggregates across customers, and no shared model or parameter is trained from customer
  data. Calibrations, models and scorecards may reference only your own uploads.
- **No vendor credentials** are accepted or stored, and the service never fetches market data on your behalf.
- **Retention.** Uploads and outputs expire after your retention period (default 30 days, configurable from 1 to
  365). Deleting a dataset removes its objects and rows and writes a tombstone (ids, counts, hashes, time; no data).
- If your vendor licence does not allow a service provider to process the data for you, use the self-hosted edition:
  it is the same software. Databento's agreement allows contractors acting for the customer but restricts
  service-bureau use of its platform; we have not obtained a vendor position on this.
- Models or reports derived from Coinbase or Tardis data are never offered by the service to anyone but the customer
  who uploaded the data.
- Do not upload Binance Vision archive files: their terms prohibit hosting. Reports of IEX HIST data must carry
  IEX's attribution line when shared; queuetruth does not add it to datasets or reports automatically.

## For firms: a derived-data register

Keep, per venue and output type: the source, the licence, the aggregation level, whether the output can be reverse
engineered into the source, and who may receive it. These are the questions an exchange's derived-data declaration
asks, and the output table above is a starting point.
